# attention-never-replaced-bite
# video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d1FrlDzGEB4

Let's break down the core idea behind
this paper. Attention is all you need.
Google Brain, 2017.
9 years old and still what almost
everything you use is running on.
9 years. Hundreds of papers built to
replace it. Linear attention, state
space models, every attention is not all
you need rebuttal on archive. And the
model you talked to this morning is
still underneath this. So this isn't a
tutorial on what attention is. It's an
autopsy of the choices that made it
survive. Punchline up front, it didn't
win by being smarter. It won because it
fit the hardware.
Here's what they were actually fighting
in 2017.
A recurrent net computes position five
from position four, which came from
three, which came from two. It's a
chain. A 40 token sentence is 40 steps
you cannot skip. Not with a bigger GPU,
not with a hundred of them. Meanwhile,
the GPU, built to do 10,000
multiplications at once, sits idle
waiting its turn. Attention already
existed bolted onto that chain. The move
was deleting the chain.
Replace it with this. Every position
looks at every other position directly.
And here's the load-bearing part. None
of those lookups depend on each other.
So the sentence isn't 40 steps. It's one
matrix multiply. Queries against keys,
softmax times values, and all 40
positions resolve in the same instant.
Recurrence needs in sequential
operations. Self-attention needs one,
however long the input. And token one
reaches token 40 in a single hop, so
gradients never run a 40 step relay.
Now a detail that looks like nothing. A
score is a dot product over 64
dimensions, and a sum like that spreads
out as dimensions pile up. Its spread
grows like the square root of DK. So
scores don't swing plus or minus one.
They swing plus or minus eight. Push
that into a soft max and you get a
spike, not a distribution. One position
at basically one, the rest at zero. And
soft max at a spike has no gradient
left. The layer stops learning where to
look. Divide by root DK and it trains.
The justification for the most copied
symbol in deep learning is,
delightfully, a hedge.
One head produces one way to average,
and an average is a blur. If a token
needs to track its subject, its verb
tense, and the bracket it has to close,
one soft max smears across all three and
you get mush. So, project into eight
subspaces of 64 dimensions, run eight
attentions side by side, concatenate.
Each head gets to be sharp about one
relationship, and the sly part is the
accounting. 64 * 8 is 512, so eight
heads cost what one did.
But deleting the chain deleted order
along with it. Attention is permutation
invariant. Shuffle the tokens and every
score is identical. The outputs just
shuffle along. The cat ate the fish and
the fish ate the cat are the same bag.
So, order gets injected by hand. Signs
and cosines whose wavelength stretch
from a few radians to 60,000. A clock
where every dimension ticks at its own
rate. Shift by a fixed offset and it's a
linear transform. That's the thread a
decade of position papers went on to
pull.
If, why does this thing refuse to die is
your kind of question, like, subscribe,
and send it to whoever told you
attention got replaced last week.
Then the boring glue that makes six
layers trainable at all. A sublayer
never replaces its input, it adds to it.
Layer norm of X plus whatever the
sublayer computed, which leaves a
straight wire from the loss down to
layer one. Instead of a gradient
fighting through six transformations to
get there, every block writes an edit
onto a running stream instead of
overriding it. That's why we call it the
residual stream, a shared bus every head
reads from and writes back to.
Now the table they were honest about.
Per layer, self-attention costs n
squared times d, recurrence costs n
times d squared. So when the sequence is
shorter than the model is wide, 40
tokens, 512 dimensions, every sentence
in 2017,
attention is genuinely cheaper. And in
the column that mattered, one sequential
step versus n, a quadratic in length
traded for a constant in depth. Back
then that quadratic was free. It stopped
being free the day context hit 64,000.
The design didn't age badly. The input
got a thousand times longer.
And the numbers that bought it the
benefit of the doubt, 28.4 BLEU on
English-German, beating every prior
model, ensembles included, by over two
points. 41.8 on English-French, a new
single model record after three and a
half days on eight GPUs. Their base
model beat everything published before
it on roughly a hundredth of the
training compute. Not just better,
absurdly cheaper.
Straight from the abstract.
So the whole recipe, cut the recurrence
and the sequence resolves in one shot
instead of n steps. Divide by root dk
and the softmax keeps a gradient. Split
the width into eight heads and one blur
becomes eight sharp relations. Add a
sinusoidal clock because order stopped
being free. Wrap it in a residual stream
so depth trains. Nine years of
challengers have attacked exactly one of
those, the n squared, and everyone that
stuck kept the skeleton.
One line. Every choice in here was
bought with parallelism. The whole
sequence computes at once, and
everything else exists to keep that
shape trainable.
Which leaves the question the whole
attention war turns on. Is the N squared
a bug we eventually delete, or is
looking at everything the thing that
actually makes it work? That's my read.
Tell me yours. Until next time.
